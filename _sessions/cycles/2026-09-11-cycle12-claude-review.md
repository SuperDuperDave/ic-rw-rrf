## Review — cycle12 public research design

**Marker: cycle12-public-review-a038344974**

**The gate, as published, conflates three separable things.**

*Case validity* is a property of the instrument, not of any policy. Does one auditable case exist where the initial evidence admits two materially different answers and an accessible record settles the distinction? That is checkable by reading the case, and it is prior to every policy question.

*A useful comparison* requires only that two acquisition policies **could** select different records under identical budget, access and tools. This is a design property, demonstrable on paper by exhibiting the divergent selections. It does not require having observed divergence.

*Demonstrated model failure* — a baseline that actually acquires the redundant record — is an outcome. Making it a precondition inverts the order of inference and reduces the study to a hunt for baseline failures, which the synthesis rightly forbids. Cycle07 and cycle11 already show the honest version: a sufficient baseline closes the experiment, and that is a result rather than a defect to be searched around.

So: no. An alternative policy need not be shown to differ empirically. It must be shown to be *capable* of differing on a valid case — a construct check, not a feasibility batch.

**On the native-label correction.** Cycle06 is a clean illustration of reconciling *existing* evidence: two materially different readings of the same run, settled by a record already present in the raw stream, with the misread `no_fallback` label corrected without launching a repair batch. It illustrates the shape of a decisive missing record. It is not model evidence — the history was solved offline, and offline replay remains distinct from a native experiment.

**One next design action.** On paper, for a single candidate case, write down the two admissible answers, the settling record, and two policies that provably diverge on it. If that cannot be written for even one case, pause here.
