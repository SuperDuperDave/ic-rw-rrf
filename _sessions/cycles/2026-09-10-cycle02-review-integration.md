## Recommendations not adopted as written

1. **Missing-outcome bounds.** Filling missing labels in both groups with 0,
   then both with 1, does not give the extreme difference between groups. The
   lower difference assigns missing supported outcomes0 and missing isolated
   outcomes1; the upper does the reverse. Compute these endpoints within query
   before averaging. The memo's pooled missing fractions describe a different
   weighting from the proposed query-average estimand.
2. **A threshold from RRF.** Rank-only RRF defines no calibrated relationship
   between a binary relevance-probability gap and a specialist weight. Its
   formula cannot supply the proposed meaningful-effect threshold without an
   additional decision/loss model. Use an explicit research decision threshold,
   label it as such, and make no NDCG implication.
3. **Uniqueness language.** Outside the retained lexical union does not mean
   only a neural source could retrieve the document. Old lists are truncated,
   and their upstream pool differs. Describe the observed access boundary.
4. **Causal language.** The absolute statement that no support intervention
   exists is too strong: recorded support can be manipulated while truth stays
   fixed to test an aggregator's response. Such an intervention does not cause
   document relevance. The chosen next test is descriptive, not causal.
5. **Power and nulls.** More judged cases permit narrower missing-label bounds,
   but raw counts or an interval containing zero do not establish adequate power
   or a negligible effect. Keep a prespecified practical band, precision target,
   uncertainty envelope, and inconclusive outcome.
