## What we adopt and what we correct

- Adopt the certificate-content direction: checking actual evidence differs
  from substituting stipulated reliability numbers into a supplied Bayes model.
  A certificate with an incorrect transition and correct final answer separates
  evidence validity from endpoint agreement.
- Keep the original consensus/minority question visible. A harder scalar task
  might induce shared failures without creating useful dissent. The research
  can study evidence verification without first finding spontaneous role errors.
- Correct “custody and novelty”: the audit establishes the declared syntactic
  exclusion, not research novelty or semantic independence. Claude did not
  inspect files or independently verify artifact hashes.
- Correct the claim that endpoint flips are uninformative about N. Changing
  only N changes the Boolean in two of these fixed pairs; it changes the final
  triple in all four. This establishes those local changes, not harder reasoning.
  No chance model was specified for the matched truths. Independent Bernoulli
  truths with probability1/3 would agree with probability5/9, but independence
  is not established here and is not an explanation of this panel.
- Narrow the cycle07 citation: all-correct responses supplied no observed error
  variation. They did not experimentally establish what happens when errors
  increase or that checkable dissent cannot occur.
- Do not adopt an undefined “materially cheaper than replay” gate. Verifying
  every step may require the same arithmetic as replay; local detectability of
  an incorrect transition is different from cheaper whole-certificate checking.
  Any efficiency claim needs its own cost comparison. Machine checking remains
  the practical ceiling for these executable examples.
- Reduce Claude's proposed eight long-program corruptions to one fresh short
  program and three certificate types. The algebra guarantees the intended
  negative control before drawing parameters; there is no search for a
  corruption that happens to preserve the answer.
