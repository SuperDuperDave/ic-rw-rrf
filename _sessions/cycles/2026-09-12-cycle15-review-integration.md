## Corrected or declined

1. **Judgment coverage was reversed.** A has the fewest explicit-qrel entries,
   260/3,000; S has the most, 280/3,000. The memo appears to have read counts
   without explicit qrels as judged counts. Their small difference does not
   establish that judgment availability cannot affect the mean gaps; relevance
   and rank discount matter. No causal judgment-bias analysis was performed.
2. **A sparsity story is not an explanation of the ties.** The packet did not
   establish the memo's query-label-count premise. Even a single-positive query
   can have fractional nDCG at ranks2–10. The observed 241 metric ties do not
   identify their cause or reduce the result to a count of binary query flips.
3. **Unidentified mechanism is not a null result.** This study cannot identify
   repetitive-agreement or minority-expertise effects. That is a limit on the
   causal question, not a demonstrated zero effect of the project's broad idea.
   Likewise, balanced A−P sign counts do not erase its measured negative mean;
   broad harm remains unestablished, while this local mean loss is observed.
4. **Proposed future patterns do not uniquely identify mechanisms.** A fixed
   lexical-family rescaling directly intervenes on relative vote weight, holding
   lists constant. A favorable outcome would show sensitivity to that choice,
   not prove correlation caused the original deficit. Failure to improve would
   not establish that content or depth is the cause. Single-lexical-plus-S arms
   also change source content and multiplicity together.
5. **Reduce the proposed experiment.** The memo names four singleton fusions plus
   one family-balanced fusion (five new systems, despite saying four). Start
   with one prospective B-versus-C weight intervention and the existing simple
   references. Further controls need a separate discriminating purpose; no
   additional experiment or performance sweep belongs in this cycle.
